How it works

One key does most of it.

Hold fn in anything you can type into: a mail draft, a chat, a terminal, a form in the browser. A small bar at the bottom of the screen shows what Super Flow is doing. The text is pasted at the cursor and your clipboard is put back afterwards.

PressWhat happensThe bar
fn hold Push-to-talk. Speak, let go, and the text appears where the cursor is.
fn+space or double-tap fn Hands-free. A locked session for longer thoughts, with live text above the bar. Press fn again, or Finish, to stop. 0:12
fn+control Command Mode. Say what to do with the selected text. More below.
⌥M or triple-tap fn Notetaker. Start or stop taking notes in a call. More below. 12:04

esc cancels · ⌃⌘V pastes the last transcript again · ⌥1 polishes the selected text. No fn key? Settings offers ⌃space and ⌃⌥space instead, and every shortcut can be re-recorded.

  1. ListenThe microphone opens when fn goes down and stays open 150 ms after you let go, so the last syllable isn’t cut.
  2. RecogniseThe speech engine transcribes while you talk, on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded.
  3. Clean upRules and a small local model remove fillers and false starts, apply your corrections, and format numbers and lists.
  4. PasteThe text goes in at the cursor and your clipboard comes back. Password fields are never read or typed into.

Cleanup

It tidies your words. It doesn’t write new ones.

Speech engines write down everything, ums included. Super Flow runs rules and a small local model over the transcript: fillers and false starts go, corrections are applied, numbers and lists are written properly, and the style follows the app. If the model rewrites, answers a question or drifts, guardrails discard its version and you get the rules-only text. “Undo AI edit” in History brings back the raw transcript.

Command Mode · fn+control

Select text. Say what to change.

Hold fn and control with text selected and say what you want: shorter, a list, another language. The local model rewrites the selection in place. Case changes and lists work even before the model has downloaded.

Transforms run a saved prompt on the selection without speaking: ⌥1 polishes it, ⌥2 and ⌥3 run prompts you write.

Command Mode is experimental. Turn it on in Settings › Experimental.

Engines and privacy

No account. No cloud. No API keys.

Speech recognition, cleanup, speaker labels and notes run on local models. Audio, transcripts, notes and settings never leave the Mac. There is no telemetry, no crash reporting and no analytics of any kind.

Developers

Swift, SwiftPM and the Command Line Tools.

Super Flow is a native macOS app under the MIT licence, with no Xcode project. FluidAudio runs Parakeet, Nemotron and speaker diarization on Core ML; whisper.cpp and llama.cpp run Whisper and Qwen. A headless self-test covers every module, including the real engines on synthetic speech and the 222-case cleanup set.

What happens on fn one module per stage

  1. InsertionKitsees fn go down through an event tap
  2. DictationKitcoordinates the session and the bar
  3. AudioKitcaptures the mic at 16 kHz, mono
  4. SpeechKitParakeet, Nemotron, Apple or Whisper
  5. CleanupKitrules around llama.cpp, with guardrails
  6. InsertionKitpastes, then restores the clipboard
  7. FlowStorewrites the row to SQLite history

Feature modules depend only on SuperFlowCore and talk through its Services registry. MeetingKit, FlowBarKit, HubKit and UpdateKit sit beside the pipeline.

Questions

Before you install.

Is it really free?

Yes. Super Flow is MIT-licensed, has no account and no paid tier, and sends no telemetry. The source is on GitHub.

Does it work offline?

Yes, once the models have downloaded. Dictation, cleanup and meeting notes never need the network.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Apple silicon gets the full stack, with Parakeet on the Neural Engine; Intel Macs dictate with Whisper on the CPU.

Why does macOS say it “cannot be opened”?

The app is signed with the project’s own identity and is not notarized. Click Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to the Super Flow message. The one-line installer clears the quarantine flag for you, so this only happens after a DMG install.

How much disk space do the models take?

On a 16 GB Mac with Apple silicon, about 1.8 GB for dictation and 2.8 GB more for meetings; on an 8 GB Mac, about 1.1 GB and 1.3 GB. They live in ~/Library/Application Support/Super Flow/Models, and Settings › Models can pause, delete or import each one.

Which languages does it understand?

Parakeet covers 25 European languages and Whisper large-v3-turbo falls back for 99. Nemotron is English only. Apple’s engine on macOS 26 covers 10 languages, one at a time. Cleanup removes fillers in French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch as well as English.

Will it record my meetings on its own?

No. When a call app picks up your microphone, the bar asks “Meeting detected — Record?” and nothing records until you click Record. You can also start and stop with ⌥M.

What permissions does it ask for?

Microphone, to listen while you dictate or record. Accessibility, to paste into the field you’re typing in, read up to 500 characters around the cursor for spelling (you can turn that off), and notice meeting apps. Input Monitoring, to see the fn key. System Audio Recording, only for the other side of a meeting. Calendars, optionally, for meeting titles. Each is asked for on its own screen when a feature needs it.

How do I uninstall it?

Run curl -fsSL https://udsy19.github.io/super-flow/install.sh | bash -s -- --uninstall, or quit the app and delete it from Applications.
