How it works

One key, any text field, anywhere on your Mac.

  1. Hold fn and talk
  2. Cleaned as it lands
  3. Works in any app

Hold fn and talk

Talk the way you would to a colleague. Pause, back up, say “no wait”. The microphone opens when the key goes down and closes the moment you let go.

Cleaned as it lands

Rules and a small local model take out the ums and false starts, apply your corrections and write numbers and lists properly. If the model strays, you get the rules-only text.

Works in any app

Mail, chat, notes, a terminal, a form in the browser. The text goes in at the cursor and your clipboard is put back. Password fields are never read or typed into.

Notes for your calls

When a call app picks up your microphone, Super Flow asks before it records. The transcript appears within a couple of seconds, and a local model writes the summary and action items.

Nothing leaves your Mac.

No account, no telemetry, no analytics. Audio, transcripts and notes stay on your disk. The app only goes online to download models and check for updates, and you can switch off both.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Questions

  • Anywhere you can type: mail, chat, notes, a terminal, a code editor, a form in the browser. Super Flow pastes at the cursor and puts your clipboard back afterwards.

  • No. Speech recognition, cleanup, speaker labels and meeting notes all run on local models. There is no account and no telemetry. The app only connects to download models and to check for updates, and both can be turned off.

  • Any Mac on macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Apple silicon runs the full stack, with speech on the Neural Engine; Intel Macs dictate with Whisper on the CPU.

  • Parakeet, the default engine, covers 25 European languages. Whisper large-v3-turbo is the fallback for 99 languages, and on macOS 26 Apple’s on-device engine is a third choice.

  • The models download once, in the background, speech first and only on unmetered networks. A 16 GB Apple silicon Mac installs about 1.8 GB for dictation and 2.8 GB more for meetings.

  • The app is signed but not notarized. Click Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway next to the Super Flow message. The Terminal installer clears that flag for you.

  • Never. When a call app picks up your microphone, Super Flow asks first. You and the other side are recorded on separate tracks, and meeting audio is deleted after 30 days; transcripts and notes stay.

  • Nothing. Super Flow is free and MIT-licensed, with no trial, no paid tier and no account. The source is on GitHub.

  • Settings › General offers ⌃Space for push-to-talk and ⌃⌥Space for hands-free, and every shortcut can be recorded again.

  • Yes. It is Swift and SwiftPM with the Command Line Tools, no Xcode project. Clone the repository, run swift build, then ./scripts/build-app.sh.
